# Saporo — Voice & Tone (observed)

Observed in "The Identity Exposure Journal" (https://www.saporo.io/resources/blog)
and site copy. These are inferences from published writing, not a style guide
Saporo has endorsed — verify with the team before treating as law.

## Voice attributes

- **Conversational, second-person openings.** Posts start by putting the
  reader in a scene: "Think back to your last identity audit. You probably
  had lists." (https://www.saporo.io/resources/blog/the-power-of-the-security-graph)
- **Short declarative contrasts.** "Attackers only need one path in.
  Defenders are expected to secure everything." (same post)
- **Position-taking, not hedging.** "Cyber resilience is not about reacting
  quickly. It is about ensuring the attack cannot succeed in the first
  place." (same post)
- **Practitioner-addressed.** Content speaks to CISOs and security
  architects in their own terms (attack paths, blast radius, chokepoints,
  Tier 0) rather than to a generic business reader.
- **Metaphor of order vs chaos.** "Bring order to identity chaos" / "We
  bring clarity to identity chaos" recur across product and company pages.

## Observed dos

- Open with the reader's lived situation, then reframe it.
- Quantify: attack paths counted, percentages of misconfigurations reduced,
  time to deploy.
- Name the adversary's perspective explicitly ("models identity connections
  from an adversary's perspective").

## Observed don'ts (inferred)

- No exclamation marks in the sampled copy.
- No generic AI hype framing; the graph/attack-path mechanism is always the
  hero, not "AI" in the abstract.

## Blog identity

- Blog name: **The Identity Exposure Journal**
- Framing: "Research, practical guidance, and field insights on preemptive
  identity security, attack paths, and continuous exposure reduction"
- Founders write posts themselves (Guillaume Eyries, Olivier Eyries bylines).
