# Research playbook: Argument

**Formats:** Thought Leadership, Best Practices.

**What the page needs.** A position the reader hasn't heard stated this clearly, argued from how things work and tested against the strongest opposing view. A best-practices page is still an argument: each practice is a claim that doing it this way beats the alternative, with the reason. Your research supplies the conventional position in its strongest form, the evidence that bears on it, and cases that test it.

## Gather this (→ `## Subject Material`)

### The conventional view
What most practitioners, vendors or analysts currently say, in its strongest form, attributed to named sources.

### Why it holds, and where it breaks
The mechanism behind the conventional view, and the conditions under which it stops being true, sourced where possible.

### Evidence that bears on it
Data, incidents or documented outcomes that support or undercut the conventional view. Record each item with what it shows **and what it doesn't show**.

### Practices (Best Practices)
Per practice: what to do, the reason it works (the mechanism), the alternative it beats, and the source or "practice".

### Cases
One or two documented cases that test the position: an incident, a published post-mortem, a public program outcome.

## Prefer these sources
Named experts' published writing and talks, standards bodies, incident write-ups, and primary data. Opinion pieces are acceptable **as the view being argued with**, attributed.

## Don't collect
- A pile of statistics that each "support" the topic's importance. This page runs on reasoning.
- Views from head-to-head competitors, even as the opposing view.

## Candidate positions for this mode
Two or three genuinely different positions, each with the strongest objection and the evidence on both sides. At least one should go against the conventional view.

## Statistics & Data Points for this mode
Zero to three, each one bearing directly on a candidate position.

## Done when
- The conventional view is stated from named sources.
- Each candidate position has its best objection and the evidence both ways.
