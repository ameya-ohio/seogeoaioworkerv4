# Research modifiers

The research brief includes the one section below that matches each of this page's facets. Each section adjusts the playbook; it doesn't replace it.

## Role: cluster

A cluster page answers **one question in depth**. Go deep on the question in the title and nowhere else. Anything the research brief lists under *Owned by other pages* gets one line at most, so a writer can summarize it and link. Don't re-research the parent topic's definition. The hub already owns it, and every cluster page that re-defines it reads the same.

## Role: hub

A hub is a **routing page**. Its reader needs to know which child page they want. Research the distinctions between the child pages (what question each one answers, and how to tell which applies) and the one framing that holds them together. Don't research each child in depth; each child page does that for itself.

## Role: pillar

A pillar covers **the whole topic**. Research breadth first (every sub-theme's core mechanism), then the connecting argument. Follow the Pillar playbook even when the article type is something else.

## Funnel: tofu

The reader is new to the problem. Research the mechanism and its consequence in plain terms, and one documented case that makes it concrete. Company material is limited to one short section after the value is delivered, so internal proof needs no research.

## Funnel: mofu

The reader is working out how to deal with the problem. Research method: how practitioners do it, what it takes, where the common approach fails, and the criteria for judging an approach. The company can serve as the worked example, so note which case study or proof point fits (from `context/`).

## Funnel: bofu

The reader is deciding. Research the decision criteria, the objections, and the proof: `context/case-studies/` and `context/sales/proof-points.md` (Citable: yes) first. External research supports the problem statement and the category comparison.

## Intent: informational

Answer the question fully and exactly. The reader wants to understand or do something, not buy.

## Intent: commercial

The reader is comparing options. Research the criteria that separate them, and evidence for each criterion.

## Intent: transactional

The reader is ready to act. Research only what removes the last objection.

## Intent: navigational

The reader wants a specific page or product. Research the facts that page must state correctly.
