# Research playbook: Data

**Formats:** Stats / Data, Original Research.

**What the page needs.** Here the numbers are the point, and the stat budget doesn't apply. Each figure still has to be exact, dated and attributed to its primary publisher, with enough methodology that the reader can judge it. The page is useful because it curates, explains and reconciles figures, not because it lists as many as possible.

## Gather this (→ `## Subject Material`)

### Datasets
One entry per primary report or dataset: publisher, date, sample (size, population, geography), how key terms are defined, and the page URL.

### Figures by question
Group the figures under the questions a reader asks ("how common is X", "how fast is Y", "what does Z cost"). Give each figure its dataset and its exact wording.

### Conflicts
Where figures disagree, and the reason (methodology, population, definitions, date).

### Trend
How the figures move across editions of the same report, cited to each edition.

For **Original Research**: the company's own dataset, from `context/` only. Record its methodology from the fact sheet and never infer beyond it.

## Prefer these sources
The primary report itself (not press coverage of it), government statistics, academic studies, and recognized annual reports from non-competitors.

## Don't collect
- Figures you can only find quoted secondhand. Follow them to the primary report or drop them.
- Competitor-published figures.

## Candidate positions for this mode
What the data, read together, says that no single report says: a trend, a reconciliation, a figure everyone misreads.

## Statistics & Data Points for this mode
List the page's central figures here, following the verification rules. The research budget ceiling still applies to attributed claims. Put the remaining figures in *Figures by question* with their dataset reference.

## Done when
- Every figure traces to a primary dataset with its methodology.
- Conflicts are explained.
