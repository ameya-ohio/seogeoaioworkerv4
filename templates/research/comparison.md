# Research playbook: Comparison

**Formats:** Comparison (Concept), Comparison (Vendor), Alternatives, Tools Listicle, Buyer's Guide.

**What the page needs.** The reader is trying to decide something: which concept applies to their situation, which approach to adopt, which tool or category to buy. The page has to give the dimensions that decide it, where each side actually differs, and when each is the right choice. Your research supplies authoritative definitions of each side and evidence for each difference that isn't self-evident.

## Gather this (→ `## Subject Material`)

### The sides
Each side's authoritative definition (from the standard, the originating framework, or the vendor's own documentation for a vendor format), and what each is for.

### Dimensions
The five to eight dimensions that decide the choice (the data it needs, who owns it, what triggers action, what it outputs, what it misses, cost and effort). For each, what each side does, with a source where the difference isn't self-evident.

### Where they overlap
Cases where the distinction collapses or both apply. This is where most comparisons go wrong.

### Choosing
The conditions under which each side is the right answer, as concrete situations.

### When confusion costs
A documented case (incident, audit finding, published post-mortem) where treating one as the other caused a failure, if one exists.

For **vendor formats** (Comparison (Vendor), Alternatives, Tools Listicle): per vendor, what its own public docs say it does, pricing model if public, and a re-verify-by date. Competitor claims come only from that competitor's own docs (the D51 exception).

## Prefer these sources
Standards and frameworks that define the terms (NIST, ISO, Gartner category definitions via their public pages), vendor documentation for vendor formats, analyst and practitioner writing that compares the two, and documented failures.

## Don't collect
- Statistics about either side's prevalence that don't bear on the choice.
- Tangential standards invoked for authority. If the source doesn't define or distinguish the two sides, leave it out.

## Candidate positions for this mode
A comparison argues about **which difference matters most, and what goes wrong when people ignore it**. The position names the dimension it rests on.

## Statistics & Data Points for this mode
One to three, each tied to a dimension.

## Done when
- Both sides are defined from authoritative sources.
- At least five dimensions are filled in, with sources where needed.
- Overlap and "choosing" situations are written.
