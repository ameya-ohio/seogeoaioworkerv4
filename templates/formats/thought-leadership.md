# Format guide: Thought Leadership

**The job.** Make an argument. Its value is a position, not information, so it's the format most likely to be quoted by name and the most worthless if hedged.

## Structure
1. **H1:** the argument as a claim, never a question ("Identity Is the Primary Attack Surface").
2. **Intro (answer block).** Built from the research Topic Summary ¶1–2 (D44); paragraph 1 carries the answer block below within its first 60–80 words, paragraph 2 the sourced why-now. The answer block asserts the thesis plainly in 50–60 words.
3. Why the conventional view persists: steelman it properly (the concede-then-pivot arc).
4. The argument: 3–5 declarative H2 sections, each one supporting pillar backed by data, incidents or named mechanisms.
5. The counter-argument, taken seriously, then answered.
6. What follows if the argument is right, and what would change the author's mind.
7. A named author byline with credentials (mandatory for this format).

This format carries **no FAQ** (D49): a forced FAQ undercuts an argument. Key Takeaways at page.md's count. The company stays implicit: the argument makes its approach the obvious conclusion without pitching. Close with page.md's CTA.

## Fails when
It's hedged into agreement, has no identifiable author, or makes a claim it doesn't evidence.
