# Format guide: Comparison (Vendor)

**The job.** Capture competitor-branded demand ("[company] vs [competitor]") and convert it. High intent, and the format most exposed to legal and reputational risk.

**Vendor format (D51).** Needs human sign-off before export. Every claim about the competitor is sourced to their own public documentation, with the URL and date checked, and re-verified by the date the outline records.

## Structure
1. **H1:** "[Company] vs [Competitor]: [honest framing]".
2. **Intro (answer block).** Built from the outline's Intro Strategy, which starts from the thesis (D60); paragraph 1 carries the answer block below within its first 60–80 words, and paragraph 2 says why it matters now: a change or a consequence that supports the thesis, with at most one figure and only one the argument turns on. The answer block says who each is genuinely best for in 60 words and **concedes something real**. That concession is what makes the rest credible.
3. At a glance: a comparison table of architecture, deployment, coverage, remediation, time to value, pricing model and best fit.
4. What the competitor does well, placed **before** the company's case and written so their customers would agree.
5. Where the company is different: architectural, not adjectival.
6. Feature-by-feature detail (tables per area), deployment and time to value, pricing approach.
7. "Choose [competitor] if … / Choose [company] if …", then the evaluation or migration path.
Then **Key Takeaways** (the count in page.md), declarative H2s throughout (D33), the company section weighted by the funnel, the **FAQ** within page.md's range, and the close with page.md's CTA.

The FAQ includes the awkward questions. No `Review` or `AggregateRating` schema on the competitor.

## Fails when
There's no genuine concession, competitor claims are unsourced, or the page is never updated.
