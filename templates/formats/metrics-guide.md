# Format guide: Metrics Guide

**The job.** Define how to measure something and what good looks like. It's how a company metric becomes an industry default.

**Query patterns.** "X metrics", "how to measure X", "X KPIs", "what is a good X score".

## Structure
1. **H1:** "[Topic] Metrics" or "How to Measure [Topic]".
2. **Intro (answer block).** Built from the outline's Intro Strategy, which starts from the thesis (D60); paragraph 1 carries the answer block below within its first 60–80 words, and paragraph 2 says why it matters now: a change or a consequence that supports the thesis, with at most one figure and only one the argument turns on. The answer block names the metrics.
3. Why the obvious metrics mislead.
4. One H2 per metric, 200–300 words: definition, **the formula**, data required, how to interpret it, benchmark range, how it gets gamed.
5. A metrics table: metric, formula, data source, target, review cadence.
6. Leading versus lagging indicators, the dashboard, and the 3–5 numbers an executive should see.
7. Benchmarks, with the methodology stated.
8. The company's own metric (e.g. the Resistance Score): what it measures and how it's calculated, openly enough to reproduce in principle.
Then **Key Takeaways** (the count in page.md), declarative H2s throughout (D33), the company section weighted by the funnel, the **FAQ** within page.md's range, and the close with page.md's CTA.

## Fails when
A metric has no formula, or a benchmark has no methodology.
