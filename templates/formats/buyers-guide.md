# Format guide: Buyer's Guide

**The job.** Be the reference a buyer uses to run the evaluation, including the RFP.

**Query patterns.** "X buyer's guide", "how to choose X", "X evaluation criteria", "X RFP".

## Structure
1. **H1:** "[Category] Buyer's Guide" (or "[Category] RFP Checklist").
2. **Intro (answer block).** Built from the outline's Intro Strategy, which starts from the thesis (D60); paragraph 1 carries the answer block below within its first 60–80 words, and paragraph 2 says why it matters now: a change or a consequence that supports the thesis, with at most one figure and only one the argument turns on. The answer block names the evaluation criteria.
3. Who needs this category and who doesn't, placed early. The disqualification section is the strongest trust signal available.
4. The market landscape by category type, not vendor name.
5. One H2 per evaluation criterion, 200–300 words: why it matters, how to test it, what a good answer sounds like, what a red flag sounds like.
6. A requirements matrix: must-have, nice-to-have, ignore.
7. Questions to ask every vendor, as a numbered list copy-pasteable into an RFP.
8. Pricing models, proof-of-concept design, build vs buy, and time-to-value expectations.
Then **Key Takeaways** (the count in page.md), declarative H2s throughout (D33), the company section weighted by the funnel, the **FAQ** within page.md's range, and the close with page.md's CTA.

The criteria can reflect where the company is strong, but each must stand as a buyer criterion on its own terms.

## Fails when
Someone buying a competitor couldn't use it. That's the bar.
