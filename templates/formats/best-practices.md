# Format guide: Best Practices

**The job.** Prescribe what good looks like, specifically enough to argue with.

**Query patterns.** "X best practices", "how to do X properly", "X recommendations".

## Structure
1. **H1:** "[N] [Topic] Best Practices".
2. **Intro (answer block).** Built from the outline's Intro Strategy, which starts from the thesis (D60); paragraph 1 carries the answer block below within its first 60–80 words, and paragraph 2 says why it matters now: a change or a consequence that supports the thesis, with at most one figure and only one the argument turns on. The answer block names and numbers the practices.
3. Why it matters: the cost of getting it wrong, reasoned from how the failure happens; a documented incident or one sourced figure where it carries the point.
4. One H2 per practice, 150–250 words, headed with the imperative: why it works, how to implement it, how to verify it, what it costs.
5. Priority order: which three to do first.
6. Anti-patterns: what to stop doing. Consistently the most-cited section of this format.
7. How to measure adoption, then how the product enforces these (map practices to controls).
Then **Key Takeaways** (the count in page.md), declarative H2s throughout (D33), the company section weighted by the funnel, the **FAQ** within page.md's range, and the close with page.md's CTA.

Best Practices explains *why*; the Checklist verifies *whether*. Keep that split clean or the two pages cannibalise each other.

## Fails when
A practice is generic enough to fit any topic ("monitor continuously").
