# How to Execute a 7-11 Touch Cadence on Every Deal With AI

The Deal Velocity Sequencer is the AI Worker that solves the math no human can. Industry research shows 7 to 11 touches over 14 to 15 days produces a statistically significant 72% lift in deal progression from first meeting to mid-funnel. Most AEs in B2B sales juggle 30 active deals at once. 30 deals × 7 touches × 14 days = 210 touches per fortnight per AE. No human runs that cadence at quality across 30 deals; the AE focuses on the four they think are about to close, the other 26 stall in stage 2 indefinitely, and pipeline rots.

This article publishes the cadence design (what 7-11 touches actually means across email, LinkedIn, and call), the AI Worker pattern that owns the cadence (the Deal Velocity Sequencer), and how the human AE plugs in without taking over the touches the AI Worker is already running.

## Key Takeaways

- The **Deal Velocity Sequencer** is the AI Worker that executes a 7-11 touch cadence over 14-15 days on every active deal in pipeline, automatically.
- The 72% deal-progression lift comes from running the cadence on every deal, not from running 12+ touches on hot ones. Coverage beats intensity.
- 30 deals × 7-11 touches × 14-15 days = 210-330 touches per AE per fortnight. Untenable for humans, perfect for AI.
- The cadence blends channels: email, LinkedIn, voicemail, video. Most teams default to email-only and lose 30-40% of effective touches.
- The AI Worker pauses the cadence on three triggers: meeting booked, AE manually disqualifies, prospect requests pause. Otherwise it runs to completion.

## The math: 30 deals × 7 touches = 210 per fortnight

The Deal Velocity Problem is fundamentally a capacity problem.

Each AE in a typical B2B sales org carries 20 to 30 active deals at any time across pipeline stages. Each deal benefits from sustained engagement at the cadence research has consistently shown to lift progression: 7 to 11 touches across 14 to 15 days following first meeting. The cadence is what keeps the deal warm between formal touchpoints, what reinforces value when budget conversations stall, what prevents the buyer from drifting to a competitor while your AE is on another call.

The arithmetic kills the human-only motion. 30 deals × 7 touches = 210 touches per AE per two-week period. At an aggressive 11-touch cadence, 330. Each touch is not just an email; it is a research check (what changed at the prospect this week?), a personalization decision (what angle for this touch?), and a multi-channel choice (email this time, LinkedIn comment next?). Even at 5 minutes per touch (which is generous given the research expected at quality), you are talking 17 to 27 hours per AE per fortnight on cadence execution alone. AEs do not have 17 hours per fortnight available outside of meetings, demos, and proposal work.

So most teams quietly accept that cadence runs on the four to six deals the AE believes are about to close. The other 24 to 26 deals get sporadic touches when the AE remembers, then go silent, then go cold. Pipeline reports show 30 active deals; the truth is 4 active deals and 26 deals waiting to be marked Closed-Lost.

## Why 7-11 touches over 14-15 days

The 7-11 touch cadence is not a marketing slogan. It is the structure consistently associated with the highest deal-progression rate in B2B benchmarks.

Saleshandy's 2026 cold email research aligns with the broader B2B cadence pattern: campaigns with 3-5 follow-up steps consistently hit 8.3% reply rates, compared to 4.1% for sequences without follow-ups. According to [Saleshandy](https://www.saleshandy.com/blog/cold-email-statistics/), 58% of replies come from step one and the remaining 42% from follow-ups. Translated into deal-cycle dynamics: half your deal momentum comes from the touches after the first meeting, not the first meeting itself.

The 14-15 day window matters because attention spans in B2B buying cycles are roughly two weeks for a stage. A deal that has not progressed in 15 days is functionally stalled; the buyer has moved on to other priorities and your competitive set is widening. The cadence is what keeps you present during those 15 days. Without it, the deal stalls; with it, you produce the 72% progression lift the EverWorker proprietary deployment data sees on customers running the full Sequencer.

The other reason the cadence works at this length: behavioral physics. The first touch is the proof you exist. The fourth touch is the proof you care about the deal. The seventh touch is the proof you understand the buyer's situation specifically. Each subsequent touch is supposed to add information, not repeat the first three. A working cadence produces sequential evidence of relevance.

## What the cadence looks like

A working 7-11 touch cadence is multi-channel and varied in shape, not seven copies of the same follow-up email.

A typical 9-touch cadence over 14 days:

- **Day 1.** Post-meeting recap email with three concrete next steps and a calendar link for the second call.
- **Day 2.** LinkedIn connection request with a one-line personalized note referencing the meeting.
- **Day 4.** Asset email: a case study or relevant resource matched to the use case discussed on the first call.
- **Day 6.** Voicemail or call attempt with email follow-up summarizing what you would have said.
- **Day 8.** LinkedIn comment or DM on a recent post by the prospect, demonstrating you are paying attention.
- **Day 10.** Multi-thread reach: outreach to a second contact in the buying group (the AE's champion's manager, or a peer in the same function).
- **Day 12.** Video walkthrough or Loom showing a relevant product capability tailored to the prospect's environment.
- **Day 13.** Direct check-in email with a low-friction question ("What would help most right now?").
- **Day 15.** Final attempt with explicit door-open language, leaving room to re-engage.

Most teams default to email-only and lose 30-40% of effective touches because the prospect mentally filters email but engages with LinkedIn and voicemail differently. The variety is the variable. Channel rotation also signals effort and prevents the cadence from feeling like spam.

The exact mix shifts by deal stage and persona. Enterprise deals weight LinkedIn and multi-thread touches; SMB deals weight email and video; technical buyers weight Loom and product walkthroughs. The Sequencer holds the structure; the AE supplies the substance for each deal.

## The Deal Velocity Sequencer

The Deal Velocity Sequencer is the AI Worker that holds the cadence structure and executes most of it automatically, with explicit handoffs back to the AE for the touches that need human judgment.

What the Sequencer does without human input:

- Schedules all 7-11 touches across the 14-15 day window the moment a deal is created, with channel and content type per day.
- Drafts each scheduled email using the Knowledge Engine's messaging assets matched to the deal's stage, persona, and use case.
- Composes LinkedIn-engagement prompts the AE can approve in one click.
- Tracks engagement on every touch (email opens are unreliable per the [infrastructure article](/blog/how-to-choose-email-infrastructure-ai-sdr), so it focuses on replies, click events, and LinkedIn engagement).
- Updates the CRM continuously: which touches have fired, which are pending, what engagement happened.
- Pauses the cadence on the three triggers covered below.

What the Sequencer hands to the AE:

- Final review of email drafts before send (with one-click approve or "regenerate" feedback).
- Voicemail and live-call scripts (the AE makes the call; the Sequencer prepares the script and the post-call email).
- Video and Loom recordings (the AE records; the Sequencer schedules the send).
- Multi-threading hand-offs (the Sequencer identifies the right additional contact; the AE decides whether to engage).

The result is that the AE's cadence work compresses from 17-27 hours per fortnight to roughly 4-6 hours, almost all of it spent reviewing and approving rather than drafting and scheduling. The 24-26 stalled deals get the same cadence as the four hot ones because the Sequencer is running it, not the AE.

According to [Salesmotion's 2026 AI SDR comparison](https://salesmotion.io/blog/ai-sdr-tools-compared), 70-80% of off-the-shelf AI SDR customers churn within months. The customers who stick are the ones whose AI handles deal execution after the meeting, not just the meeting-booking. The Sequencer is the layer most off-the-shelf AI SDRs do not include.

## How the AI Worker knows when to pause

The Sequencer pauses the cadence on three explicit triggers and one implicit signal:

**Meeting booked.** When a meeting gets scheduled in the calendar, the Sequencer pauses pending touches automatically. After the meeting, the AE chooses to resume the cadence or end it depending on the meeting outcome. This is the most common pause trigger.

**AE manual disqualification.** The AE marks the deal Closed-Lost or Disqualified in the CRM. The Sequencer respects the field change immediately and stops all pending touches.

**Prospect-requested pause or unsubscribe.** The Sequencer's reply-handler watches for "not now," "send to my colleague," "remove me," and similar phrases. Detected pause requests stop the cadence; detected unsubscribes close the deal as Lost-Disqualified with the reason logged.

**Implicit signal: zero engagement after touch 7.** If the prospect has not opened, clicked, or replied through the first 7 touches, the remaining 4 touches typically get pulled forward as low-frequency check-ins rather than stopped entirely. The data ([Instantly's 2026 Cold Email Benchmark Report](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026) shows reply rate distribution skews late) suggests touches 8-11 still produce some replies, but at higher cost-per-touch. Most teams default to running them; some teams pull the plug at touch 7.

The architectural detail that matters: the Sequencer reads CRM property changes in real time. Stage moves, status field updates, calendar events, and sequence statuses all feed back into the cadence loop. This is one of the reasons the [CRM foundation](/blog/how-to-set-up-crm-for-ai-workers) matters so much; without enforcement on the relevant fields, the Sequencer's pause triggers fail.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a 7-11 touch cadence?

A 7-11 touch cadence is a structured sequence of 7 to 11 outreach touches across multiple channels (email, LinkedIn, voicemail, video) executed over 14 to 15 days following an initial sales conversation. Industry research consistently associates this cadence length with a 72% lift in deal-progression rate from first meeting to mid-funnel. The 7-touch floor is the minimum; the 11-touch ceiling avoids fatigue. The window length matters because B2B buying-cycle attention spans are roughly two weeks per stage.

### Why does the cadence need to run on every deal, not just hot ones?

Because pipeline reports lie about which deals are actually hot. AEs reliably believe they know their top four deals; the deals that close are usually a different set, and a meaningful share are deals the AE wrote off two weeks ago. Running the cadence on every deal produces the coverage that surfaces real engagement signals. The Deal Velocity Sequencer enables this because the human AE could not run 30 cadences in parallel, so they pick four. Pick fewer, miss more.

Most teams default to email-only and lose 30-40% of effective touches because the prospect mentally filters email but engages differently with LinkedIn and voicemail. Channel variety also signals effort and prevents the cadence from feeling like spam. The exact channel weighting shifts by deal stage and persona: enterprise weights LinkedIn and multi-thread touches; SMB weights email and video; technical buyers weight Loom and product walkthroughs.

The Deal Velocity Sequencer compresses cadence work from 17-27 hours to 4-6 hours of review-and-approve.

Prospect-requested pause or unsubscribe: the reply-handler watches for "not now," "send to my colleague," "remove me," and similar phrases.
