# How to Prosecute Every Inbound Lead Within 5 Minutes

Speed to lead is broken at most B2B sales orgs, and the math is not subtle. Most teams prosecute about 20% of the leads marketing generates in any given quarter. The other 80% get a generic 2-percent-converting sequence or nothing at all. That is pipeline left on the table every single quarter, and it is not a marketing-quality failure or a sales-effort failure. It is a human-capacity failure.

The 5-minute response window matters because conversion drops sharply with delay. Industry research (the InsideSales.com Lead Response Management Study, classic 2007 finding still cited through 2026) shows leads contacted within 5 minutes convert at roughly 21 times the rate of leads contacted at 30 minutes. The math has not changed in 18 years. What has changed is that AI SDRs make 5-minute response possible at 100% prosecution rate, which is the architectural pattern this article publishes.

## Key Takeaways

- **Speed to lead** is the time elapsed between an inbound lead's signal (form fill, demo request, content download) and the first qualified human or AI touch. The benchmark is under 5 minutes.
- About 80% of inbound leads at most B2B orgs are not properly prosecuted because human SDR capacity does not scale to the volume marketing produces.
- Leads contacted within 5 minutes convert at roughly 21 times the rate of leads contacted at 30 minutes (InsideSales.com Lead Response Management Study).
- An AI SDR achieves 100% prosecution rate by classifying signal + intent within seconds, executing the Universal Research Protocol, and enrolling in the right sequence with no human bottleneck.
- Performance benchmarks for a working AI SDR on inbound: demo request up to 35%, webinar attended around 15%, content download 5-10%, blended 5-15% conversion to meeting.

## Why 80% of inbound leaks

Every inbound lead is a clock starting. The clock is the prospect's attention span and the buying-cycle window in which they are actively evaluating solutions. Every minute that passes between a form fill and the first response is a minute the prospect's attention drifts toward competitors, alternatives, or the calendar invite they were about to send.

The 80% leak shows up the same way at most B2B orgs. Marketing produces 1,000 leads in a quarter from inbound channels (events, webinars, content, paid, organic). Sales has 3 SDRs each booking 15 to 20 meetings a month, prosecuting maybe 200 of those leads at quality. The remaining 800 either get a generic templated sequence that converts at 2% or get dropped into a "we'll get to them" nurture campaign that effectively means dropped.

This is not a sales-effort problem. SDRs spend 28% of their week selling and the other 72% on research, list-building, and admin. Even if you doubled their selling time, you would prosecute maybe 400 of the 1,000 leads. The capacity floor is too low. According to [Saleshandy's 2026 cold email statistics](https://www.saleshandy.com/blog/cold-email-statistics/), 71% of buyers cite irrelevance as the top reason they do not respond. Generic templated sequences sent to lukewarm leads after a 24-hour delay are exactly the irrelevance the data describes.

## The 5-minute response standard and the 21x stat

The 5-minute window is not a marketing slogan. It is what the data says.

The InsideSales.com Lead Response Management Study (2007, replicated multiple times since) found that contacting an inbound lead within 5 minutes is roughly 21 times more effective at conversion than contacting at 30 minutes. The same study showed contact attempts within the first hour produce conversion rates 7 times higher than contact attempts in the second hour. Drift's 2019 State of Conversational Marketing replicated the pattern with chat-based inbound. Multiple HubSpot and Salesforce-internal studies have confirmed the same window through 2026.

Why 5 minutes? Buyer psychology and competitive density. The buyer who just downloaded your whitepaper or filled your demo form has 3 to 7 active vendor evaluations in flight. The first vendor to engage with relevant context wins disproportionate share of the conversation. The 30-minute response curve is the curve of the buyer talking to a competitor, getting pulled into a meeting, finishing their next task, or simply forgetting they filled the form.

The other thing the data shows: contacts attempts beyond the first hour are essentially noise. The conversion rate at hour two is roughly the same as the conversion rate at day one, which is roughly the same as the conversion rate at week one. If you do not respond in the first hour, the timing variable stops mattering. That is why the 80% leak hurts so much; it is the difference between hitting the 21x window and hitting the noise floor.

## How an AI SDR achieves 100% prosecution

An AI SDR achieves 100% prosecution by removing the human capacity bottleneck. Every inbound lead is classified, researched, and routed within seconds rather than minutes or hours. The architectural pattern (covered in detail in [how to build an AI SDR that actually converts](/blog/how-to-build-ai-sdr)) runs four steps in sequence on every inbound:

1. **Signal classification.** Demo Request, Webinar Registered or Attended, High-Intent Page Visit, Content Download, Direct Contact Form, Trial Signup. Each signal type has a default intent strength.
2. **Intent classification.** The AI SDR combines signal type with contact data (firmographic tier, persona fit, ICP geography) to assign one of 5 intent categories: High Intent Active Evaluation, Functional Intent Solution Evaluation, Medium-High Category Awareness, High Intent Pricing Interest, or Low Fit Nurture.
3. **Research protocol.** The Universal Research Protocol runs the equivalent of 20 minutes of research per lead in seconds. Visit the company website, pull the last 12 months of news, read the contact's LinkedIn, identify the personalization angle, query the knowledge base for the right messaging asset.
4. **Sequence enrollment.** High-Intent classifications enroll in same-day sequences with personalized first-touch within minutes of the form fill. Functional Intent gets 24-hour follow-up. Medium-High gets 24-hour. Low Fit routes to nurture.

The entire chain runs without a human touching the lead unless and until a reply comes in. That is what produces 100% prosecution: there is no triage queue, no "we'll get to them," and no 24-hour delay while the SDR finishes their morning sequence.

The 5-minute window is not a stretch goal at this point. It is a default. The harder problem is making sure the first-touch email is good enough to convert at the 21x rate, which is what the four-component AI SDR architecture and the four-step instruction framework solve. According to [Salesmotion's 2026 AI SDR comparison](https://salesmotion.io/blog/ai-sdr-tools-compared), off-the-shelf AI SDRs see 70 to 80% early-customer churn within months because they hit the 5-minute window with thin instruction sets that produce generic emails. Speed without quality is just faster spam.

## Speed-to-lead vs lead routing

These are different problems, easy to conflate.

**Lead routing** is the rules-based assignment of an inbound lead to the correct human SDR or AE based on territory, vertical, account ownership, or round-robin distribution. Routing happens at most CRMs in seconds. The lead arrives in the assignee's queue.

**Speed to lead** is the time from form fill to the first qualified outbound touch from the assigned rep. Routing is fast; the assigned rep responding is the slow step. Most teams' speed-to-lead is 4 to 24 hours despite routing being instantaneous, because the human SDR is busy with their morning sequence, in a meeting, or working through their queue from the previous day.

The AI SDR collapses the gap between routing and response. The lead is routed (or in many architectures, classified directly without routing), and the AI SDR's first-touch goes out within minutes. The human SDR's role shifts from triage and first-touch to handling replies, qualifying conversations, and booking the meeting. That role is appropriate for human capacity at the volume marketing actually generates.

## How to measure prosecution rate

Prosecution rate is the percentage of inbound leads that receive a qualified, timely first-touch response. The simple formula:

> **Prosecution rate = (Leads that received qualified response within 5 minutes) ÷ (Total inbound leads in the same period)**

Most teams have never measured this directly because the data has historically been hard to collect. With an AI SDR plus a properly configured CRM (covered in [how to set up your CRM for AI Workers](/blog/how-to-set-up-crm-for-ai-workers)), the measurement becomes a CRM report:

- Filter all inbound leads created in the last 30 days.
- Cross-reference against the AI SDR's sequence enrollment timestamp.
- Calculate the percentage where (enrollment - lead creation) is under 5 minutes.

A working AI SDR shows 100% on this metric (or very close, with the small variance accounting for system processing time). A team running on human SDRs typically shows 15 to 30%. The gap is the leak. For context on the conversion benchmarks the AI SDR is sold against, [Instantly's 2026 Cold Email Benchmark Report](https://instantly.ai/cold-email-benchmark-report-2026) puts the average outbound reply rate at 3.43% and top performers at 10%+. A four-component AI SDR on inbound should comfortably outperform those outbound benchmarks by lead source.

The other measurement that matters is conversion-to-meeting per intent classification. Demo requests should convert at up to 35% to first meeting. Webinar attendees around 15%. Content downloads 5 to 10%. If your AI SDR is hitting 100% prosecution but the conversion rates are below those benchmarks, the issue is the four-step instruction framework, not the speed-to-lead architecture.
